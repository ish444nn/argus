## The layering stage

Money laundering is conventionally described in three stages. Placement
introduces criminal proceeds into the financial system. Layering separates
those proceeds from their origin through successive movements. Integration
returns the value to the holder in an apparently legitimate form.

Layering is the stage most visible in transaction data, because it consists
almost entirely of transfers. Its purpose is to lengthen and complicate the
audit trail so that tracing funds back to the predicate offence becomes
impractical. The transfers need not be complicated individually; the
obstruction comes from their number, and from each hop being separately
plausible.

## Chained pass-through transfers

The simplest layering structure is a chain: value enters an account or address,
leaves almost immediately for the next, and repeats. Each hop holds the value
only briefly and adds no economic activity of its own.

Analytically, a chain is a path of nodes with a single input and a single
output. The length of the run is what carries information, not any single hop.
An isolated pass-through is unremarkable and extremely common in ordinary
activity; a sustained run of them, where each intermediate step exists only to
forward value onward, is the shape layering produces. Interpretation should
therefore rest on the length of the chain rather than on the presence of
pass-through behaviour by itself.
