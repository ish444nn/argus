## Consolidation as a laundering step

A funnel account receives value from many unrelated sources and forwards it,
usually promptly and usually onward to a single destination. It is the mirror
image of structuring: where structuring fragments value to stay beneath
attention, funnelling reassembles fragments once they have been sufficiently
separated from their origin.

FinCEN described the pattern in the context of accounts receiving deposits in
many locations with withdrawals concentrated elsewhere, where the geographic
and numerical mismatch between inflow and outflow was the signal rather than
any individual deposit.

## Distinguishing it from ordinary aggregation

Many legitimate participants aggregate: exchanges, payment processors,
merchants receiving customer payments, and pooled settlement arrangements all
collect from many sources by design. The pattern alone is therefore weak
evidence and must be read against context.

What distinguishes suspicious consolidation is the absence of a plausible
aggregating role, a short holding period between inflow and outflow, and
onward movement that continues rather than terminating in an identifiable
economic use. Where the sources themselves show signs of having been fed by
fragmentation, the combination of fan-in preceded by fan-out is considerably
more informative than either shape alone.
