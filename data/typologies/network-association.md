## Proximity as information, and its limits

That a transaction is connected to one already established as illicit is
genuine information. Laundering networks reuse infrastructure, and
counterparties are rarely independent of one another. Financial intelligence
practice treats a confirmed link to known illicit activity as one of the
stronger single indicators available.

The strength of the inference depends entirely on how the status of the
neighbour was established. A counterparty confirmed through investigation or a
filed report supports a much stronger claim than one that is merely also under
suspicion. Chains of suspicion resting on other suspicions decay quickly, and
treating an unreviewed alert as though it were a confirmed finding manufactures
certainty that does not exist.

## Recording the basis of the link

Any assertion that a transaction associates with illicit activity should record
which counterparty, in which direction value moved, and on what basis that
counterparty is regarded as illicit. Without those three the claim cannot be
checked, and an analyst inheriting the case cannot tell whether they are
looking at evidence or at an echo of an earlier alert.
