## Controlled groups of counterparties

FATF work on professional money laundering describes organisations that provide
laundering as a service, operating pools of accounts and instruments used
interchangeably across clients. Because the pool is under single control, value
circulates within it before leaving.

The observable consequence is a group of counterparties that transact with each
other far more than unrelated parties would. Ordinary economic activity tends
to produce sparse local structure: the parties one deals with usually have no
particular reason to deal with each other. Controlled pools produce the
opposite.

## Interpreting local density

Density among the counterparties of an entity is therefore worth measuring,
with two cautions. Some legitimate structures are also densely connected,
including settlement systems, market-making arrangements and activity within a
single platform. And density is scale-dependent: among a handful of
counterparties it carries little information, since a small group can be fully
connected by chance.

Density is most informative when it appears alongside other indicators,
particularly when the dense group also shows the short holding periods and
absent economic purpose characteristic of layering.
