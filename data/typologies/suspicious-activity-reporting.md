## What suspicion requires

FinCEN advisories describe red-flag indicators while stressing that the
presence of an indicator does not by itself establish suspicion. The
determination rests on the whole picture: indicators observed, context
available, and any explanation the activity admits.

The advisory on convertible virtual currency lists indicators observable
without customer information, including transfers through many addresses in
quick succession with no apparent economic purpose, interaction with addresses
connected to previously identified illicit activity, and patterns consistent
with the use of obfuscation services.

## Separating observation from conclusion

The practical discipline the advisories impose is separating what was observed
from what is inferred. An indicator is an observation. Suspicion is a
conclusion, reached by a person, that the observations together admit no
satisfactory innocent explanation.

Systems that surface activity for review should therefore present observations
and their basis, leaving the conclusion to the reviewer. Language that collapses
the distinction, describing activity as laundering rather than as consistent
with a laundering typology, misstates what the underlying data can support.
