## What structuring is

Structuring is the deliberate division of a single large transfer into many
smaller ones so that no individual movement attracts the scrutiny a single
large one would. The term smurfing describes the same behaviour carried out
through numerous intermediaries acting in concert, each handling a fraction of
the total so that no participant appears individually significant.

The defining characteristic is that the fragmentation serves no economic
purpose. Legitimate payments are split for operational reasons that leave
traces: instalments follow a contract, batches follow a settlement cycle.
Structured payments are split against thresholds, and the resulting pieces are
similar in size, close in time, and reassemble into a coherent total.

## How it appears in a transaction network

In a transaction graph the pattern is a one-to-many expansion: value arrives at
a point and immediately departs across a fan of outputs substantially wider
than the input. The outputs frequently resemble one another and differ from the
surrounding population.

Two qualifications matter for interpretation. First, high output counts are
ordinary in some settings, so the shape is only meaningful relative to the
population it sits in. Second, structuring is a placement and layering
technique rather than an end in itself, so a fan-out is usually one step of a
longer sequence, and its significance rises when the recipients themselves
continue moving value onward rather than holding it.
