## Placement

Placement is the introduction of criminal proceeds into the financial system,
and it is conventionally the stage at which the launderer is most exposed. The
funds are closest to their origin and the point of entry is where controls are
most concentrated. Techniques accordingly aim to minimise the visibility of
entry, including fragmentation across many small movements and the use of
intermediaries who introduce funds on behalf of the launderer.

## Integration

Integration returns laundered value to its owner in a form that appears
legitimate, through purchases, investments, loans or apparently commercial
receipts. By this stage the audit trail has usually been broken, and detection
depends on inconsistency between apparent wealth and known circumstances rather
than on the transfers themselves.

## Why the stage matters for interpretation

Placing an observed pattern in the cycle constrains what can reasonably be
claimed about it. A structural pattern in transfer data almost always sits in
the layering stage, where the activity consists of movement between accounts.
Conclusions about placement or integration usually require information about
the origin or destination of the value that transfer data alone does not
provide, and asserting them from structure is an overreach.
