## Proportionate allocation of attention

The first FATF Recommendation requires countries and institutions to identify
and assess their money-laundering risks and to apply resources in proportion to
them. The principle is that finite scrutiny should be directed where risk is
highest, rather than spread uniformly.

For a monitoring system this has a direct consequence. Review capacity is
fixed, so the task of the system is to order activity by risk and to make the
ordering defensible, rather than to attempt exhaustive coverage.

## Documenting the basis of a decision

The approach also requires institutions to document the basis on which risk
decisions are taken. Applied to an automated alert, this means recording what
was observed, which model produced the score, and what the assessment rests on
in a form a reviewer can follow.

The requirement is procedural rather than technical, but it constrains system
design: an alert that cannot be explained cannot support a documented decision,
regardless of how accurate the underlying model is.
